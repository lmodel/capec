# Explanations

* [Keys and values](keys-and-values.md) - Why the schema's keys and values are spelled as they are: each key is the XSD's own element or attribute name, such as `Related_Attack_Patterns`, each value the XSD's own text, and classes and enums carry a `Capec` prefix.
* [From XML to data](xml-to-data.md) - Why a converter stands between the catalogue and the schema, and its rules: MITRE publishes XML, LinkML validates keys and values, and `scripts/capec_to_json.py` turns one into the other without changing a key or a value. A test turns the data back and finds the catalogue.
* [Identifiers and references](identifiers-and-references.md) - Why a CAPEC number is a string of digits and why references are typed: a number names and never counts, and the rdflib dumper cannot expand a bare identifier, so RDF comes through JSON-LD. `Exclude_ID` ranges over any entry, because the XSD says only that it names an ancestor.
* [Why abstraction levels are values](abstraction-levels.md) - Why Meta, Standard and Detailed are values of `Abstraction` and not subclasses: no field depends on them. The XSD says an execution flow is usually for Detailed patterns, and CAPEC 3.9 gives flows to all three levels, so no rule ties them.
* [Rules in Python](rules-in-python.md) - Why `tests/capec_rules.py` exists: a JSON Schema looks at one value at a time, so it cannot say that a reference names an entry the catalogue holds, or that no number is used twice. What the data merely shows goes in `tests/test_catalogue.py` with its counts.
* [Faults in the catalogue](catalogue-faults.md) - The oddities of CAPEC 3.9 that `tests/test_catalogue.py` pins: CAPEC-244 cites `REF-97`, which the catalogue lacks, two patterns repeat a weakness, two names end in a space, two skills hold no text, eleven flows repeat a step number, and CAPEC-699 is a Meta child of a Standard pattern.
* [Mappings](mappings.md) - Where the schema crosses to other vocabularies: STIX, ATT&CK, CWE, the SEPSES CAPEC ontology, SKOS, Dublin Core, schema.org and Wikidata. The schema is the one home for mappings, and each is read at its source and held by a test.
* [What the generators do with these keys](generator-limits.md) - How LinkML's generators treat the XSD's short keys: `gen-owl` warns that six keys shared across classes are ambiguous and gives them no label, the JSON-LD context gives a key one IRI, and the key `Date` shares a docs page with LinkML's type.
* [What the schema leaves out](scope.md) - What the schema does not do: it says nothing about whether an entry is true, nothing evaluates a view's filter, and it holds the keys the XSD defines even where CAPEC 3.9 uses none, such as `Audience` and `Member_Of`.
